# Platforms: long-form posts on LinkedIn and X

The method is the same on both: one payload, built to be saved, 250–400 words. What changes is the limit, the fold, formatting and what each feed rewards. Platforms change these often; anything marked (verify) should be checked against the platform's current help pages before you rely on it.

## LinkedIn

- **Length limit.** About 3,000 characters per post (verify). A 400-word post is usually 2,200–2,600 characters, so the 250–400 word range fits with room to spare.
- **The fold.** Roughly the first two to three lines, about 200 characters on desktop and fewer on mobile, show before "…see more" (verify). The hook has to land there; see `long-form-text-hook`.
- **Formatting.** Plain text. There's no native bold or italics; "bold" Unicode characters from converter tools are read badly by screen readers and search, so skip them. Line breaks and blank lines are kept. Dashes and numbers for lists.
- **Links.** Posts with an external link in the body are widely reported to get less reach (verify). That's why the skill allows one link at most and moves extras to the first comment.
- **Hashtags and tags.** Optional, and a few at most. Tagging companies pulls readers off the post.
- **What it rewards.** Dwell time, saves, and comments from people in the poster's network are widely reported to drive distribution (verify). A post that holds the reader long enough to work through a checklist fits that well.

## Long X posts

- **Length limit.** 280 characters for standard accounts. Paid (Premium) accounts can post up to about 25,000 characters (verify). Without a paid account, a long post has to become a thread (see below).
- **The fold.** The timeline cuts long posts after roughly the first 280 characters with "Show more" (verify). The hook and its proof have to sit above that cut.
- **Formatting.** Paid accounts can use bold and italics in long posts (verify). Default to plain text anyway so the same draft works on LinkedIn; add emphasis only if the user asks.
- **Links.** Posts with external links are widely reported to get less reach (verify). Put the link in a reply to the post if reach matters more than clicks.
- **What it rewards.** Replies, reposts and bookmarks, especially in the first hour, are widely reported to drive reach (verify). Bookmarks map directly to the save score.
- **Tone.** Blunter and faster than LinkedIn. Cut warm-up sentences, and keep the setup to two lines or fewer.

## If the user has no paid X account: thread it

Split the post into a thread instead of cutting it:
- Post 1 is the hook, with its claim and proof, under 280 characters.
- Each payload step becomes its own post, numbered, so each one can be read (and screenshotted) alone.
- The takeaway gets its own post. The CTA and link go in the last post.

## Posting the same piece on both

- Write the LinkedIn version first; it's the stricter on formatting.
- For X, cut the setup to its shortest form and check that the claim and proof sit in the first 280 characters.
- Keep the payload identical. It's the part that earns the save on either platform.
