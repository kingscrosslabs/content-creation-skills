# Platforms: hooks for LinkedIn and long X posts

The hook is whatever shows before the "see more" fold, so the fold is the one platform detail that changes how a hook is written. The method doesn't change: full sentences, credibility or a contrarian claim, and a stated takeaway. Platforms change their limits often; anything marked (verify) should be checked against the platform's current help pages before you rely on it.

## LinkedIn

- **The fold.** The feed shows roughly the first two to three lines, about 200 characters on desktop and fewer on mobile, before "…see more" (verify). Line breaks use up the visible space fast, so a hook spread over several short lines shows less of itself.
- **What that means for the hook.** The claim and the proof have to land in about the first 150 characters, so they survive on mobile. The promised takeaway can sit just past that if it has to, but the reader should know the topic before the fold.
- **What it rewards.** Dwell time and comments from people in the poster's professional network are widely reported to drive distribution (verify). The "see more" click is itself a signal that the post is worth reading (verify).
- **Tone of the feed.** Professional identity is on display. People share what makes them look informed or generous to colleagues, which is why the shareability score leans on identity.

## Long X posts

- **Length.** Standard accounts are capped at 280 characters. Paid (Premium) accounts can post much longer, up to about 25,000 characters (verify).
- **The fold.** Long posts are cut off in the timeline after roughly the first 280 characters with a "Show more" link (verify). The hook is everything above that cut.
- **What that means for the hook.** You have a little more room than on LinkedIn, but the opening sentence still does most of the work, because X readers scroll fast and judge on the first line. Lead with the number or the claim, not the setup.
- **What it rewards.** Replies, reposts and bookmarks, especially in the first hour, are widely reported to drive reach (verify). Bookmarks map directly to this skill's "built to be saved" goal.
- **Tone of the feed.** Blunter and faster than LinkedIn. A contrarian claim lands harder; corporate phrasing reads as an ad.

## Writing one hook for both

If the user posts on both, write the hook to the tighter fold (LinkedIn mobile): claim and proof in the first ~150 characters. It will then work on X as well. Adjust tone in the post, not the hook.
